# Reflection

## 1. Decisions

One of the biggest decisions I made during this project was choosing to build a budgeting website focused on college students and young adults. What made me choose this topic is the fat that financial literacy is something that affects almost everyone, especially people who are just starting to manage money on their own. I wanted to create something that felt useful and realistic instead of just making a random website with no real purpose behind it, and something that I would use myself.

Another important decision was keeping the project simple and beginner-friendly. Early on, I considered adding more advanced features, but I realized that making the website clean, organized, and easy to understand was more important than making it overly complicated. I focused on creating clear navigation, readable content, and tools that users could actually understand and use. I also decided to include a budget calculator because it added interactivity to the site and made the project feel more practical.

I also spent time deciding on the overall design and layout. I wanted the site to look professional while still being approachable. I used a consistent color scheme, matching headers across pages, organized navigation tabs, and card-style sections to make the website feel modern and easy to follow.

## 2. What Worked

The part of the project that worked best was the overall structure and organization of the website. Once I had the navigation system and CSS styling set up correctly, it became much easier to build additional pages while keeping everything visually consistent. I am pretty satisfied with how the final design looks compared to where I started.

The budget calculator also turned out better than I expected. It calculates income, expenses, and remaining balance while giving users feedback on whether they are within budget or over budget. Adding buttons, result messages, and responsive layouts made the website feel much more interactive and complete.

I am also happy with how the educational content turned out. The Budget Basics, Savings Tips, and Sample Budget pages all support the overall purpose of the website and make it feel like more than just a simple calculator. Instead, it feels like a beginner resource for people trying to improve their financial habits.

## 3. What I Would Do Differently

If I started this project over, I would spend more time planning the layout and file structure before I began coding. At the beginning, I focused mostly on getting pages working, but later I had to go back and reorganize CSS styles, navigation bars, and headers to make everything consistent. Having a stronger plan from the start would have saved time later in the process.

I would also probably learn more JavaScript before starting the project. While I was able to build the calculator successfully, I realized that interactive features require more planning and troubleshooting than basic HTML and CSS. If I had more experience with JavaScript, I think I could have added more advanced features like expense tracking, savings goals, or charts.

Another thing I would change is experimenting with frameworks or tools that speed up design and responsiveness. Building everything manually helped me learn, but it also showed me how much work goes into maintaining consistency across multiple pages.

## 4. What I Learned

This project taught me a lot about building for the web and how important organization is in web development. I learned how HTML, CSS, and JavaScript work together to create both the structure and functionality of a website. I also learned how important consistency is when designing multiple pages. Small details like matching headers, navigation bars, colors, and spacing make a huge difference in how professional a website looks.

I also learned that web development involves a lot of problem solving. Many times, small mistakes in CSS or HTML caused layouts to break or styles to disappear, and I had to carefully troubleshoot the issue. Through that process, I became much more comfortable reading code and figuring out what needed to change.

Finally, I learned that building a website is a process of continuous improvement. My original version was very basic, but over time I improved the design, added features, reorganized the layout, and made the site more user-friendly. Seeing the progress from the beginning of the project to the final version gave me a better understanding of both the technical side of web development and the importance of planning, iteration, and attention to detail.
