What is a Budget?

A budget is a plan for how you will use your money. It helps you track your income, control spending, and make intentional financial decisions. Instead of guessing where your money goes each month, a budget gives you a clear picture.

Why Budgeting Matters

Budgeting is one of the most important financial skills you can develop. It allows you to stay in control of your finances, avoid overspending, and prepare for unexpected expenses. For students and young adults, budgeting can reduce stress and build long-term confidence with money.

Income vs. Expenses

The foundation of any budget is understanding the difference between income and expenses.

  • Income: Money you receive, such as paychecks, allowances, or side income.
  • Expenses: Money you spend on things like rent, food, gas, and entertainment.

A successful budget ensures that your expenses do not exceed your income.

Needs vs. Wants

One of the most important budgeting skills is learning to separate needs from wants.

  • Needs: Essential expenses like housing, food, utilities, and transportation.
  • Wants: Non-essential items like eating out, subscriptions, or entertainment.

Understanding this difference helps you prioritize your spending and make better financial choices.

Simple Budgeting Tips

  • Track your spending regularly
  • Start with realistic goals
  • Review your budget each month
  • Adjust when needed—budgets are flexible
